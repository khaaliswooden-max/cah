# Glossary

## Critical Access Hospital (CAH) Terminology

A comprehensive reference for terminology used in the CAH Transformation Engine. Terms are organized alphabetically within categories.

---

## Regulatory & Compliance

### CAH (Critical Access Hospital)
A designation under 42 CFR § 485, Subpart F for rural hospitals meeting specific criteria: ≤25 acute care beds, average LOS ≤96 hours, ≥35 miles from nearest hospital (or state waiver), and 24/7 emergency services. CAHs receive Medicare cost-based reimbursement.

### Conditions of Participation (CoP)
CMS requirements that healthcare facilities must meet to receive Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement. CAH-specific CoPs are found in 42 CFR § 485.601-485.647.

### Cost-Based Reimbursement
Medicare payment methodology for CAHs where reimbursement equals 101% of reasonable costs for inpatient and outpatient services. This fundamentally differs from the Prospective Payment System (PPS) used for other hospitals.

### Distance Requirement
CAH certification criterion requiring location ≥35 miles from the nearest hospital, measured by primary road. State Flex programs may grant waivers based on geography, weather, or travel time.

### Length of Stay (LOS) Limit
CAH regulatory requirement that the average length of stay for acute care patients not exceed 96 hours (4 days) annually. Calculated across all acute admissions.

### Necessary Provider Designation
Historical CAH certification pathway (no longer available for new designations after 2006) that exempted facilities from the distance requirement if the state determined them "necessary" for community access.

### Swing Bed
Acute care beds that can be used for either acute or skilled nursing facility (SNF) level care. CAHs can provide post-acute care in swing beds without separate SNF certification.

## Financial Metrics

### Bad Debt
Patient accounts receivable deemed uncollectible after reasonable collection efforts. Distinguished from charity care (never expected to pay) and contractual adjustments.

### Case Mix Index (CMI)
A measure of patient complexity based on diagnosis-related groups (DRGs). Higher CMI indicates more complex, resource-intensive patients. CAH median CMI is typically lower than PPS hospitals.

### Cost Report (CMS 2552-10)
Annual Medicare cost report submitted by all Medicare-certified hospitals. CAHs report on Form CMS-2552-10. Primary source for financial benchmarking data.

### Days Cash on Hand
Financial metric: cash and investments ÷ (operating expenses - depreciation) × 365. Indicates ability to cover operations without new revenue. CAH healthy range: 60-120 days.

### Denial Rate
Percentage of claims initially denied by payers. Calculated as: denied claims ÷ total claims submitted. CAH benchmark: <5% (higher indicates revenue cycle issues).

### Net Patient Revenue
Total patient revenue minus contractual adjustments, charity care, and bad debt. The actual revenue realized from patient care services.

### Operating Margin
(Operating revenue - operating expenses) ÷ operating revenue. Primary profitability metric. CAH benchmark: 0-4% is sustainable, <-3% indicates distress.

### Payer Mix
Proportion of revenue from different payer sources (Medicare, Medicaid, commercial, self-pay). CAH typical: 55-75% Medicare, reflecting elderly rural populations.

## Clinical Operations

### Average Daily Census (ADC)
Average number of inpatients at a census time (typically midnight) over a period. Calculated as: patient days ÷ days in period. CAH typical ADC: 8-15.

### Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA)
Federal law requiring hospitals with emergency departments to provide screening and stabilization to all patients regardless of ability to pay. Applies to all CAHs.

### HCAHPS (Hospital Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems)
Standardized patient experience survey. CAHs report on a reduced measure set. Scores increasingly tied to quality reporting requirements.

### Observation Status
Outpatient classification for patients receiving monitoring and treatment but not formally admitted. Important distinction for billing and patient cost-sharing.

### Patient Days
Total days of inpatient care provided. Calculated as sum of (discharge date - admission date) for all patients. Primary volume metric for inpatient utilization.

### Transfer Agreement
Formal arrangement between a CAH and a higher-acuity facility for patient transfers. Required by CoP for cases exceeding CAH capabilities.

### Transfer Rate
Percentage of patients transferred to other facilities. High transfer rates (>10% for non-OB) may indicate insufficient local capabilities or appropriate triage.

## Technical & Informatics

### CCDA (Consolidated Clinical Document Architecture)
HL7 standard for structured clinical documents. Common format for health information exchange, particularly for care summaries and referrals.

### EHR (Electronic Health Record)
Digital version of a patient's chart. CAHs typically use systems designed for smaller facilities (MEDITECH, Cerner, Epic Community Connect, Athena).

### FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources)
HL7 standard for exchanging healthcare information electronically. FHIR R4 is the current production version. Increasingly required for regulatory compliance.

### Health Information Exchange (HIE)
Infrastructure enabling electronic sharing of health information across organizations. CAH participation rates are lower than PPS hospitals due to cost and connectivity.

### HL7 v2
Legacy healthcare messaging standard for system-to-system communication. Still dominant in practice despite newer standards.

### Interoperability
Ability of different information systems to exchange and use data. CAHs typically have limited interoperability due to resource constraints.

### Meaningful Use (Promoting Interoperability)
CMS program requiring demonstration of EHR use for quality improvement. Now called "Promoting Interoperability" with modified requirements for CAHs.

### USCDI (United States Core Data for Interoperability)
Standardized set of health data classes and elements for nationwide exchange. Current version: USCDI v3.

## Workforce & Staffing

### FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)
Standard measure of staffing. One FTE = 2,080 hours/year (40 hours × 52 weeks). Part-time employees expressed as fractional FTEs.

### Locum Tenens
Temporary physician coverage, typically for 2-12 weeks. Common in CAHs due to recruitment challenges. Higher cost than permanent staff.

### On-Call Coverage
Physician availability requirement. CAH emergency departments require physician on-call 24/7, though not necessarily on-site.

### Scope of Practice
Legal definition of what a healthcare professional can do. Important for mid-level providers (NPs, PAs) whose scope varies by state.

### Travel Nursing
Temporary nursing staff from agencies. CAH travel nurse dependency typically 20-40% of nursing FTEs, driven by recruitment challenges and flexibility needs.

## Quality & Performance

### Core Measures
CMS-defined quality metrics hospitals must report. CAHs have a reduced, voluntary reporting program (CAH Quality Reporting).

### Never Events
Serious, preventable adverse events that should never occur. Examples: wrong-site surgery, retained surgical items.

### Process Measure
Quality metric measuring whether a recommended action occurred (e.g., "aspirin given at arrival for AMI"). Distinguished from outcome measures.

### Outcome Measure
Quality metric measuring end results of care (e.g., mortality rate, readmission rate). Generally more meaningful but harder to attribute to facility actions.

### Risk Adjustment
Statistical method to account for patient population differences when comparing quality metrics. Essential for fair CAH comparisons given different patient populations.

## Organizations & Programs

### CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services)
Federal agency administering Medicare, Medicaid, and related programs. Sets CAH regulatory requirements and reimbursement policies.

### Flex Program (Medicare Rural Hospital Flexibility Program)
Federal program providing grants to states for CAH improvement. Supports quality improvement, operational efficiency, and community engagement.

### HRSA (Health Resources and Services Administration)
Federal agency focused on improving healthcare access for underserved populations. Administers programs supporting rural health.

### NRHA (National Rural Health Association)
Advocacy organization for rural health. Provides resources, education, and policy advocacy for CAHs.

### State Office of Rural Health (SORH)
State-level agencies coordinating rural health programs. Key partners for CAH technical assistance and grant programs.

## Mathematical & Modeling Terms

### Constraint
A limitation or boundary condition in an optimization problem. CAH regulatory requirements (25 beds, 96-hour LOS) are hard constraints.

### Lagrangian Multiplier (Shadow Price)
The marginal value of relaxing a constraint by one unit. In CAH context, answers "what is the value of one additional bed?"

### Objective Function
Mathematical expression being maximized or minimized. CAH objectives might include margin, quality scores, or access metrics.

### Sensitivity Analysis
Systematic examination of how model outputs change when inputs vary. Critical for understanding which factors most influence outcomes.

### State Variable
A variable describing system status at a point in time. Examples: current census, cash position, staffing level.

## Project-Specific Terms

### Learn-Scale-Disrupt (LSD)
The iterative methodology used in this project: Learn (hypothesis and gap quantification) → Scale (algorithm development and validation) → Disrupt (implementation and measurement).

### MV-CAHI (Minimum Viable CAH Infrastructure)
Reference architecture defining median CAH capabilities. All solutions must be viable within MV-CAHI constraints.

### Performance Gap
The difference between current state (S₀) and target state (S*). Decomposed into addressable and non-addressable components.

### Three-Layer Architecture
The system design pattern: Foundation (data extraction) → Integration (normalization and compliance) → Insight (analytics and decision support).
